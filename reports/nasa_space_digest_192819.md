### 1. Astronomy Picture of the Day (APOD)
* **Title:** M74: A Grand Design Spiral
* **Date:** August 30, 2026
* **Image Link:** [M74 Spiral Galaxy (Standard)](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/image/2608/m74_hst_960.jpg) | [M74 Spiral Galaxy (High-Res)](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/image/2608/m74_hst_1280.jpg)

**Scientific Summary:**
The Astronomy Picture of the Day features **M74**, one of the most photogenic face-on spiral galaxies in our night sky. Located approximately 32 million light-years away in the constellation Pisces, this "island universe" contains roughly 100 billion stars. 

Classified as an **Sc galaxy**, M74 is a textbook example of a "grand design" spiral. Its graceful, sweeping arms are traced by bright blue star clusters and dark, winding lanes of cosmic dust. This sharp composite image was constructed from archival Hubble Space Telescope data, spanning about 30,000 light-years across. It incorporates exposures recording emissions from hydrogen atoms, which highlight the reddish glow of the galaxy's massive star-forming regions.

### 2. Earth Polychromatic Imaging (EPIC)
* **Date of Imagery:** July 30, 2026 (One month prior to reference date)
* **Spacecraft:** NOAA DSCOVR (Deep Space Climate Observatory)
* **Instrument:** Earth Polychromatic Imaging Camera (EPIC)
* **Primary Image Identifier:** `20260730000830`
* **Centroid Coordinates:** Latitude: 10.80° N, Longitude: 170.72° W (Pacific Ocean region)

**Scientific Summary:**
Captured from the Earth-Sun Lagrange Point 1 (L1), approximately 1 million miles away, the EPIC camera provides a unique, continuous natural-color view of the fully illuminated Earth. The primary image captured on July 30, 2026, at 00:03:42 UTC, centers over the Pacific Ocean, showcasing complex atmospheric cloud patterns and the vast oceanic expanse. These daily global views are critical for monitoring Earth's climate, aerosol levels, ozone, and cloud dynamics.
