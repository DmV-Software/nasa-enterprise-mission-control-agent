Here are 5 confirmed exoplanets, each discovered using a different scientific method:

### 1. Kepler-186f
*   **Discovery Method:** **Transit Photometry**
*   **How it works:** This method searches for a temporary dip in a star's brightness, which occurs when an orbiting planet passes (transits) between the star and the observer, blocking a tiny fraction of the starlight.
*   **About the planet:** Discovered by NASA’s Kepler Space Telescope in 2014, Kepler-186f was the first validated Earth-sized planet orbiting in the habitable zone of another star (an M-dwarf star about 580 light-years away).

### 2. 51 Pegasi b (Dimidium)
*   **Discovery Method:** **Radial Velocity (Doppler Spectroscopy)**
*   **How it works:** As a planet orbits a star, its gravity pulls on the star, causing the star to "wobble" slightly. This movement shifts the star's light spectrum—blue-shifting as it moves toward Earth and red-shifting as it moves away. 
*   **About the planet:** Discovered in 1995 by Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz (who received a Nobel Prize for the discovery), 51 Pegasi b was the first exoplanet ever found orbiting a Sun-like star. It is a "Hot Jupiter" located about 50 light-years away.

### 3. HR 8799 b
*   **Discovery Method:** **Direct Imaging**
*   **How it works:** Astronomers use powerful telescopes equipped with coronagraphs or starshades to block out the overwhelming glare of the host star, allowing them to capture the actual infrared light emitted by the planet itself.
*   **About the planet:** Discovered in 2008 using the Keck and Gemini observatories in Hawaii, HR 8799 b is a massive gas giant. It was part of the first multi-planet system to be directly imaged.

### 4. OGLE-2005-BLG-390Lb
*   **Discovery Method:** **Gravitational Microlensing**
*   **How it works:** This method relies on Einstein's General Theory of Relativity. When a foreground star passes precisely in front of a more distant background star, its gravity acts like a magnifying glass, brightening the background star. If the foreground star has a planet, the planet's gravity adds a secondary, smaller spike to the brightness.
*   **About the planet:** Discovered in 2005 by the OGLE collaboration, this cold, rocky/icy "super-Earth" orbits a red dwarf star near the center of the Milky Way, about 21,500 light-years from Earth.

### 5. PSR B1257+12 b (Draugr)
*   **Discovery Method:** **Pulsar Timing**
*   **How it works:** Pulsars are rapidly spinning neutron stars that emit highly precise, regular beams of radio waves. If a planet orbits a pulsar, its gravitational tug causes slight, predictable variations in the arrival times of these radio pulses.
*   **About the planet:** Discovered in 1992 by Aleksander Wolszczan and Dale Frail, this is one of the very first exoplanets ever confirmed. It is an extremely low-mass planet (about twice the mass of our Moon) orbiting a dead, highly radioactive stellar remnant.
