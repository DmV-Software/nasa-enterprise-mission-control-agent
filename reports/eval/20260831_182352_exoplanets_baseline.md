Here are 5 confirmed exoplanets and the methods used to discover them:

1. **HD 209458 b (Osiris)** – **Transit Method**
   * *Description:* Located about 159 light-years away in the constellation Pegasus, this was the first exoplanet observed transiting its parent star. It was also the first exoplanet to have its atmosphere analyzed, revealing the presence of sodium, water vapor, and carbon dioxide.

2. **51 Pegasi b** – **Radial Velocity Method**
   * *Description:* Discovered in 1995, this was the first confirmed exoplanet found orbiting a sun-like star. It is a "hot Jupiter" located roughly 50 light-years away in the constellation Pegasus. (The discoverers, Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz, won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2019 for this find).

3. **Kepler-22b** – **Transit Method**
   * *Description:* Discovered by NASA's Kepler Space Telescope, this planet is located about 600 light-years away. It holds historical significance as one of the first planets discovered in the habitable zone (often called the "Goldilocks zone") of a sun-like star, where liquid water could potentially exist.

4. **HR 8799 b** – **Direct Imaging**
   * *Description:* HR 8799 b is one of four massive giant planets orbiting the young star HR 8799, located about 129 light-years away. It was discovered using direct imaging, meaning astronomers were able to take an actual picture of the planet by blocking out the blinding light of its host star using a coronagraph.

5. **PSR B1257+12 b (Draugr)** – **Pulsar Timing**
   * *Description:* Located about 2,300 light-years away in the constellation Virgo, this was part of the very first confirmed discovery of exoplanets (found in 1992). Instead of orbiting a normal star, these planets orbit a pulsar (the rapidly spinning corpse of a dead star). Astronomers discovered them by measuring tiny, highly regular anomalies in the timing of the pulses of radiation emitted by the pulsar.
