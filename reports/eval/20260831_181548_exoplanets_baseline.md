Here are 5 confirmed exoplanets and the methods used to discover them:

1. **HD 209458 b (Osiris)** 
   * **Discovery Method:** Transit Method
   * **Details:** Located about 159 light-years away in the constellation Pegasus, it was one of the first exoplanets discovered by observing the periodic dimming of its host star as the planet crossed in front of it. It was also the first exoplanet whose atmosphere was detected.

2. **51 Pegasi b** 
   * **Discovery Method:** Radial Velocity Method (Doppler Spectroscopy)
   * **Details:** Located about 50 light-years away in the constellation Pegasus, this was the first exoplanet discovered orbiting a Sun-like star (back in 1995 by Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz, who won a Nobel Prize for it). It is a classic "hot Jupiter."

3. **Kepler-22b** 
   * **Discovery Method:** Transit Method
   * **Details:** Discovered by NASA’s Kepler space telescope, this planet is located about 620 light-years away. It is notable for orbiting within the habitable zone of its sun-like star, making it a key early milestone in the search for potentially Earth-like worlds.

4. **HR 8799 b** 
   * **Discovery Method:** Direct Imaging
   * **Details:** Located about 133 light-years away, this giant exoplanet is one of several orbiting the young star HR 8799. Because the planets are massive and orbit relatively far from their host star, astronomers were able to take actual pictures (direct images) of them using advanced ground-based telescopes equipped with adaptive optics.

5. **PSR B1257+12 b (Draugr)** 
   * **Discovery Method:** Pulsar Timing
   * **Details:** Located about 2,300 light-years away, this was actually part of the **very first confirmed discovery of exoplanets** (found in 1992 by Aleksander Wolszczan and Dale Frail). Instead of orbiting a normal star, these planets orbit a pulsar—the rapidly rotating, dead remnant of a massive star—by measuring precise irregularities in the timing of the pulsar's radio pulses.
